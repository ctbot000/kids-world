# Kids World

A cozy 3D block-building island game for kids, to play together in the browser. Build with toy bricks, raise hills, put up huge tents, plant fruit trees, grow carrots, strawberries and pumpkins on a farm of your own, dig for jewels in the mines, invite bunnies and ducks to move in, bring a pet of your own everywhere you go, ride ponies, elephants, unicorns and dolphins, drive a car, a boat, a digger and a mine cart, scoot about on a kick scooter, take your friends along in a bus or a ferry, fly a helicopter and a hot-air balloon, sit down to a tea party together, play mini-games together at a video arcade, collect capsule toys from gashapon machines, play the piano, watch TV, draw pictures together on a drawing board, visit your friends' islands, free adventure islands from grumpy monsters (giant mosquitos among them, darting down out of the air) together, and build towers to keep them off the Star Stone. Everything is made of little studded bricks.

**Play now:** <https://ctbot000.github.io/kids-world/>

![Three friends on an island: a fox saying "Let's build!", a waving bunny and a dancing panda, next to a fountain, a lamp post, a house, a flower garden and a rainbow](docs/screenshot.jpg)

1. **🔑 Log in**, or **✨ Make my login** if you have none yet: nobody plays without one, so the title screen has only those until you do (logging in needs the [island keeper](#the-island-keeper) online; once you are logged in on a device, playing there never does).
2. Press **Make an island**, pick a kind of island (Sunny, Snowy, Candy, Volcano, Fun Park or Flat Land) and how big it is (Cozy, Big or Huge), and name it: type any name, or roll one with 🎲. Turn on **⚔️ Adventure** for monster camps to free with friends, **🗼 Tower defense** for waves of monsters to see off with towers, or **👾 Monsters** if you want some about.
3. Tell a friend your six-digit **island code** (it's at the top of the screen). They press **Visit a friend** and type it in, or pick your island from **🌍 Islands open now** under it. Up to 8 players can be on one island together.
4. Build together. Your island is saved on your device, and **My islands** opens it again later. While the [island keeper](#the-island-keeper) is online, it keeps a copy too, and logging in on another device brings you and your islands there.

## What you can do

- **Build** with 16 colours of toy bricks, plus grass, sand, snow, wood, glass, lamps, star blocks, tent cloth, cookies, candy canes, flowers and more. There are over 90 blocks and plants in the toy box.
- **Tools:** Build, Pick up, Paint and Hills (raise, dig or flatten the land), each in three sizes, and Undo.
- **Interior design:** the toy box's 🛋️ **Home** tab has carpets in four colours, a wood floor, checked tiles and four wallpapers, and furniture: a chair, a rocking chair that rocks gently back and forth with you in it, a desk chair on five little wheels, a sofa, a bed, a bookshelf, a kitchen counter, a stove, a fridge, three TVs, a broadcast station, a computer glowing with code, a school desk, a chalkboard, a drawing board, a study desk with drawers, a shelf of books, a desk lamp that lights up and a chair (sit at it to study: see **Studying**), a fireplace that glows, a picture for the wall, a table, a tea table laid with a teapot, cups and cookies, a pet bed, a round basket with a soft pink cushion for your pet to curl up in (see **A pet of your own**), a hot tub, a round wooden barrel of steaming water that glows to light the room at night, a floor lamp, a potted plant, an electric fan whose head sweeps slowly from side to side and a ceiling fan with a light under it to put up against the ceiling, their blades going round, a fish tank on a cabinet with a castle, waving water plants, bubbles and five bright fish swimming round under its lamp, a round fish bowl of goldfish on a stand, a cuckoo clock for the wall, its pendulum swinging and a little bird popping out of its door on the hour, and a tower clock, a block with a lit face on every side to put on top of a clock tower; clocks' hands tell the island's time of day. Furniture turns to face you as you put it down (a picture faces out from the wall you put it on), and sofas, kitchen counters and tables side by side, or beds one behind the other, join up into one long one. Light and the view go round it, and you can stand on it; lamps and the fireplace light up the room at night. Walk up to a chair or a sofa someone is not already in and tap 🪑 **Sit** beside it (or press <kbd>Q</kbd>) to sit on it, facing the way it faces, or to a bed and tap 🛏️ **Lie down** to snuggle up in it, eyes closed (curled up on your side in a bed one block long, stretched out in two or more joined); walk, jump, <kbd>Shift</kbd> or <kbd>Q</kbd> gets you up again, in front of it, and friends see you sit and lie down. In a desk chair, walking rolls it gently along the floor instead of getting you up — a slow little ride that coasts to a stop, turns the way you steer and leaves the chair where you got off (only where you may build, for it is carried along as it goes) — and <kbd>Shift</kbd> or <kbd>Q</kbd> gets you off beside it. Sit down at a tea table while a friend sits at one too, and it's a tea party, with hearts over the table. Slip into a hot tub and the warm water comes up to your shoulders, with wisps of steam rising off it; two fit in one side by side, and soaking with a friend shows hearts over the tub. Put down ten pieces for the 🛋️ Home Sweet Home sticker, sit down once for 🪑 Comfy Spot, and sit down to tea with a friend for 🫖 Tea Party.
- **Studying:** sit down at a Study Desk and your workbooks open: ➕ adding, ➖ taking away, ✖️ times tables, ➗ sharing and 🔤 spelling (hear the word, see its picture and a sentence, and spell it). It is real practice that remembers what you know: new facts are shown to you first, then you type the answers; a miss shows the right answer and a way to work it out (make ten first, or the times fact before it), and comes back a few questions later. Each fact you get right first time goes up a box, a miss goes back to the first, and four boxes up it is known by heart; every study asks the ones you know least, a few new ones and one you know by heart, so it does not slip away. Your notebook stays on the device you study on; what you learn earns XP and the 📚 Bookworm, 💡 Know It by Heart (25) and 📝 Star Student (100) stickers.
- **Stamps** put down a whole build in one tap: a cozy house (furnished inside), a castle tower, a bouncy castle, a huge circus tent and camping tent, a rainbow, a fountain, a sail boat, a snowman (with a carrot nose, coal eyes, stick arms and a top hat), a flower garden, a vegetable patch, a picnic, a tea party, a school, an office (a wood-floored room with desks and computers, wheeled chairs to scoot between them, bookshelves, a wall TV, lamps and a plant) and more.
- **Animal friends:** bunnies hop, chicks peck, sheep graze and ducks paddle, leaving little ripples that spread out behind them on the water. Up in the air, birds flit between the treetops, the roofs and the grass, owls sleep in a tree all day and come out at night, bees go from flower to flower, seagulls circle high over the beach and butterflies flutter. After sundown, fireflies come out over the flowers and the open grass, softly blinking as they drift on the breeze (just to look at), and fade away again at dawn. Pet them for hearts. Give one a fruit or a vegetable and it follows you around, and a flying friend sits on your head when you stand still. With the Animals tool you can invite new friends to move in. (The giant mosquitos that used to drone about the meadows are monsters now: see **Monsters**.)
- **A pet of your own:** pick one in **🐾 My pet** (on the title screen, or in ⚙️ Settings on an island): a puppy, a kitten, a bunny, a hamster, a piglet, a duckling, a parrot or a baby dragon, in the colour you like, with any name you type (or roll one with 🎲). It wears a collar the colour of your T-shirt and comes along to every island you go to, yours or a friend's, and friends see it too. It trots along behind you the way you went, runs to catch up, hops up steps and swims after you; when you stop it comes to sit beside you, looking up at you, lies down after a while and falls asleep at night — and if you have put down a 🛏️ Pet Bed (in the 🛋️ Home tab) nearby, it walks over to the nearest one, curls up on its cushion and sleeps there instead, getting up when you walk off or morning comes. A parrot sits on your head when you stand still (beside you if a bird is already there), and a baby dragon flies round you and lands beside you. Say something in 💬 Talk and a parrot squawks it back a moment later in a little speech bubble of its own — a parrot that is asleep, or left far behind, stays quiet. On a ride it rides along with you, behind you on the animal or in the vehicle, and hops down when you get off. Left far behind, or stuck somewhere it cannot follow, it pops over to you. It does what you do: wave and it begs, dance and it spins round, cheer or clap and it jumps, giggle and it rolls over. Tap anyone's pet to pet it, or give it a fruit or a vegetable from your basket, and its owner hears who did. While you choose in My pet, it sits in front of you for you to see.
- **Sea creatures:** schools of fish dart about the shallows (one jumps out now and then), dolphins leap out of the sea, a whale swims out in the deep and blows water out of its blowhole, crabs walk sideways along the beach, turtles bask on the sand and paddle in the sea, and an octopus sits on the sea floor and changes colour when you pet it. Giant clams rest on the sandy floor of the shallows: tap one and its shell gapes open — sometimes a pearl pops into your basket, sometimes nothing but bubbles — and it shuts itself again after a few minutes, ready for another try. Swim out to meet them; fed a fruit, a sea creature follows you as far as the water goes. Snowy islands have a little colony of penguins, which waddle, slide on their tummies and dive and leap in the sea, and seals, which lie about on the shore, clap their flippers, play with a ball and swim.
- **Big animals to ride:** ponies gallop about and cows graze, an elephant flaps its ears and a giraffe looks over the treetops, and a big friendly dog bounds after them, tongue out and tail wagging (it woofs when you pet it); snowy islands have reindeer (one now and then with a shiny red nose) and polar bears that love a swim, and candy islands have unicorns. Walk up to one and tap **Ride** beside it (or press <kbd>Q</kbd>): you steer it as you walk, faster than you can run, and it jumps higher than you can (the elephant sprays water from its trunk instead), swims, and climbs out onto the bank. Swim out to a dolphin or the whale to ride them too: a dolphin leaps out of the sea with you on its back, and the whale blows water over you. The saddle is the colour of your T-shirt, and friends see you ride. <kbd>Q</kbd> or 👋 **Get off** (or <kbd>Shift</kbd> or ⬇️, on land) gets you down beside it, where it stays a while; fed a fruit, a big animal follows you too.
- **Vehicles to drive:** every island has a toy car, a speedboat by the shore, a yellow digger and a kick scooter, and every mine has rails down the middle with a mine cart at its doorway. Walk up to one and tap **Drive** beside it (or press <kbd>Q</kbd>), and steer it as you walk. The car is the fastest thing on land and hops up a step; the boat goes anywhere there is water, and never out of it; jump honks the horn (the mine cart rings its bell), and everyone hears it. On the kick scooter you stand on the deck, one foot planted and the other kicking along: it is nimbler than the car and quicker than you run, hops the little steps by itself and leans into its turns, and jump rings its bell (its grip pad turns the colour of your T-shirt). A 🛷 **sled** in the toy box's 🚗 Vehicles tab is for the snow: put one down, hop on and push along — on snow it glides faster than you run and slides down slopes all by itself, coasting a way on the flat before it stops; on grass it is slow going and soon stops. You sit with your legs out in front, and pull back to brake. A 🚐 **paint van** in the 🚗 **Vehicles** tab paints the ground as you drive: pick a colour with the 🖌️ **Paint** tool (a cube block in your hotbar slot), bring a van, drive round and it rolls a road or a field of that colour behind it, a few blocks wide — and undo takes the whole trail away at once. It paints only the natural ground, never anything anyone built, planted or furnished, the same rule the digger's drill follows the other way round. A 🚜 **lawn mower** in the 🚗 **Vehicles** tab mows as you drive: the grass it rolls over turns to a short mown stripe a few blocks wide, and the plants in its path pop off, the flowers into your basket as 💐 **Flowers** (put one down as a bunch, or sell them in the 🛒 shop). It leaves alone anything anyone built, rails, sprouts, farmland and its crops. It mows only the grass that grows there by itself — never anything anyone built or planted, nor the farmland, nor the crops growing in it — and jump honks like the car. The digger digs through the ground it drives into, three blocks wide and three high: hold jump to dig up a step at a time, or <kbd>Shift</kbd> or ⬇️ to dig down, and any jewels it digs out go in your basket. It never digs through anything anyone built. A mine cart rolls along its rails, round corners and up and down slopes, and on by itself when you let go, faster downhill; pull back to stop it or go the other way, and at a fork it goes the way you push. Put down more **Rails** from the toy box (they join up by themselves, and a rail a step higher makes a slope), and bring more vehicles from its 🚗 **Vehicles** tab. <kbd>Q</kbd> or 👋 **Get out** gets you out beside it, where it stays; tap a vehicle and it honks.
- **Riding together:** every island also has an open-top bus near where everyone comes in and a ferry out on the sea, each with six seats for friends behind the driver's. The first one on drives; everyone else walks up and taps **Hop on** (or presses <kbd>Q</kbd>) to sit in the next free seat, and rides along wherever the driver goes, seen in their seat by everyone. Riding along, jump honks too, and <kbd>Q</kbd> or 👋 **Get out** gets you out beside it. When the driver gets out, the others stay in their seats until somebody else takes the wheel. Each seat turns the colour of the T-shirt of whoever sits in it.
- **Flying:** every island also has a helicopter and a hot-air balloon near where everyone comes in. Get in and hold jump to take off and fly up, steer as you walk, and hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> or ⬇️ to come down; let go of both and it hovers, and it lands when it comes down onto the ground (or floats, onto water). The helicopter is quick, its rotor whirring, with a seat for two friends behind the pilot; the balloon drifts slowly, its burner roaring as it climbs, with room for four friends in its basket. Friends hop on the way they do in the bus. Get out up in the air and you fly, and the helicopter or the balloon you left comes gently down by itself.
- **Elevators:** put down **Elevator** pads from the toy box (Building) in a column, one above the other, or use the 🛗 **Elevator Tower** stamp. Stand on a pad and jump to ride up to the next pad above, or press <kbd>Shift</kbd> (⬇️) to ride down to the one below; you glide through anything in between, and friends see you go.
- **Trampolines:** put down **Trampoline** blocks from the toy box (Building), or use the 🤸 **Bouncy Castle** stamp, a floor of them inside soft walls. Jump on one and you bounce back up, lower each time until you stop; hold jump and you bounce higher and higher instead, up to eight blocks high. <kbd>Shift</kbd> (⬇️) stops you on the next bounce. Friends see you bounce.
- **Doors:** put an **Oak**, **Walnut** or **Blue Door** from the toy box (Building) in a gap in a wall. Walk up to it and swing at it (<kbd>X</kbd>, or the 👊 button, which shows up in front of a door) and it swings open for you to walk through; the next swing shuts it. Anyone may open and shut a door, even where only the island's owner builds, so nobody is ever stuck in or out, and friends see it swing. Undo takes a door you put down away, open or shut. Two doors of the same wood placed one above the other join into one tall door, the way beds join end to end: one swing of it opens or shuts the whole thing, and picking it up (or Undo) takes it away whole. - **Video arcade:** put down an arcade machine from the toy box's 🛋️ Home tab (👾 **Bop-a-Blob**, 🃏 **Picture Pairs**), or a whole arcade of six in a lit hall with the 🕹️ **Video Arcade** stamp. On Bop-a-Blob, grumpy blobs pop out of nine holes faster and faster for thirty seconds — tap one to bop it (a golden one is worth three), all of you racking up one score together with your own bops counted beside it. Join in any time (a friend arriving mid-round just picks up a controller), a good round pays out a few 🪙 coins, and the games are never saved: an island opened again starts with none.

Come within a couple of steps of a hider with nothing solid in between (window glass hides nobody) and they are found, with a 💥 over them for everyone to see. While you look, hiders' name tags, their pets and their dots on the maps are hidden from you, so they hide for real. The round is over when everyone is found or the time is up; a hider never found earns 🪙 3 coins, and the seeker a coin for each friend found. A bar over the hotbar shows the count, the time left and how many are found; the seeker or the island's owner can 🏳️ stop a round, and it ends if the seeker leaves. - **🌋 Volcano islands:** a new kind of island. The ground is dark basalt, the trees are the burnt dead trunks of the forest that was (save one grove the fire never reached, by where everyone comes in), the sea round it is lava, lakes of lava sit at the mountain's foot, and in the mountain's summit a crater of lava glows in a rim of rock, lighting the night for miles. Monsters come after you up there too, but bump the dragon instead of you: it has **120 hearts of its own**, shown by your HP, which come back once no monster is near, and when they run out it sets you down unhurt and rests a few seconds before it can be ridden again. ## Two ways to play together

**Peer to peer (the hosted page).** The browser that makes an island *is* the host: it runs the island, and friends connect to it directly over WebRTC data channels. The free [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com/) server only introduces the browsers to each other; when a direct connection is blocked, traffic goes through the TURN servers in PeerJS's default configuration. The host keeps the tab open while friends play (the island keeps running in a background tab). The island is saved in the host's browser, and reopening it from **My islands** brings it back under the same code, so friends can reconnect.

To use your own signaling server, run a [PeerServer](https://github.com/peers/peerjs-server) (`npx peer --port 9000`) and open the game with `?signal=http://localhost:9000/`. Invite links carry the setting along.

**Dedicated server.** `npm start` serves the game and hosts islands itself over WebSocket, so nobody's browser has to stay open. The page notices the server and switches to it automatically. It serves the game's files with [serve-static](https://github.com/expressjs/serve-static), speaks WebSocket with [ws](https://github.com/websockets/ws), and runs anywhere Node 22+ runs. Islands with nobody on them are kept for two hours. It also keeps the one public adventure island everyone conquers together, stage after harder stage each day (see [🌟 The shared isle](#what-you-can-do)). Once it is set up, the same command also runs the [island keeper](#the-island-keeper).

```bash
npm install
```

```bash
npm start
```

Then open <http://localhost:8747/>. To play with others on your network, `npm start -- --host 0.0.0.0` prints the addresses to share; `PORT` and `HOST` work too. Add `?p2p=1` to use peer-to-peer play even when the server is there.

## The island keeper

A computer of your own can keep a copy of every player's islands and of who they are, so nothing is lost when a browser is cleared or a tablet is replaced. The shared isle being a world of its own, it keeps each player's world there too — their own level, stickers, coins, treasures and gear on it — the same way, beside the rest. Players keep using the hosted page: whenever the keeper is online, their browsers send it what changed since last time, peer to peer, the way friends visit an island. While it is away, the copies wait in each browser for next time.

To make your computer the keeper, once:

```bash
npm run keeper-setup
```

This creates the keeper's peer id and key pair in `~/.kids-world` (or the folder in `--data` or `KIDS_WORLD_DATA`), and writes the public half to `public/keeper.json`, which is how pages find the keeper. Commit and deploy that file. From then on `npm start` puts the keeper online for as long as it runs, and <http://localhost:8747/admin/> shows what it has kept: each player with their stickers and basket, and their islands drawn from above (folded away until opened, the ones said goodbye to folded again inside), with a download of any day's copy and deletes, and where each was last seen from (the IP address their connection uses, a local one for a player on the same network, an IPv6 one with its IPv4 address too when the page offers one or the address carries one, and roughly where a public one is, looked up offline with geoip-lite so no address leaves the computer), and each one's other world on 🌟 Adventure Isle folded away under their islands (its own level, stickers, coins and what they did there), and takes a player out of the ranking or puts them back. A downloaded island opens in the game with **My islands → 📂 Open an island file**. Its parts are tabs: **Overview** (the totals and recent activity), **Players**, **Visitors** (the visitors' log, see below), and **TV broadcast**, **AI friend** and **CTB** (with the problem reports, its tab counting the open ones) while there is something to show there; the tab picked stays in the address (`#players`, say), so a reload opens it again. CTB has tabs of its own: **Workers** (its settings, the workers and what happened lately), **Tasks** (the task board, proposals, pull requests and a new task), **From players** (their ideas and the problem reports), **Model runs** and **Tokens**, kept in the address too (`#ctb/runs`). It is a plain, dense console for grown-ups, not part of the game: the system's own font, no emoji, light or dark as the computer is. The admin pages answer only the computer they run on.

- **Reaching it.** The keeper joins the PeerJS signaling service under the peer id in `keeper.json`, and answers data connections with [node-datachannel](https://github.com/murat-dogan/node-datachannel) (WebRTC for Node, with ready-built binaries for macOS, Linux and Windows). It needs no open ports, no tunnel and no account anywhere.
- **Trusting it.** Anyone can take that peer id while the keeper is away, so a page sends nothing until the keeper has signed the page's fresh random number with the private key whose public half is in `keeper.json`. The data channel is encrypted end to end, as every WebRTC channel is.
- **What it keeps.** Each island's latest copy, and one copy a day for the last 30 days it was played; each player's name, look (with their pet), basket, coins, potions and gear, what they've done and their stickers. Never the tokens that let players back into islands, nor their settings. A browser files its copies under a hash of a secret only it knows, so nobody can overwrite anyone else's. Copies come only from a page logged in: of the others the keeper keeps nothing, only a **visitors' log** (`visitors.json` in its data folder, the latest 500 devices) of each one that came by: when, how many times, the IP address and roughly where, and its page's address (the names in it, never what they say, as an island's code can be there), browser, screen and language. The admin page shows it under **Visitors**, where **Clear log** empties it. It stops taking copies at 2 GB.
- **Keeping it up to date.** The keeper runs the code of the folder it was started from, so when the game changes, update that folder (`git pull`) and restart `npm start`. A keeper running older code does not know what newer pages ask for, like the ranking: the page says the keeper needs an update, and the keeper's terminal says which request it did not know.
- **Its key.** The private key is in `~/.kids-world/keeper.json`: keep that file private, and back it up with the copies. `npm run keeper-setup -- --new` makes a new one; pages then send nothing until the new `keeper.json` is deployed.
- **Logins.** 🔑 **Log in** → **Make my login** turns the copies a browser has sent into a player you can log in as: a username, 2 to 32 letters, numbers, signs or spaces in any language (one per login, matched in any case and any width of letters, so "Minji", "MINJI" and "ＭＩＮＪＩ" are one username; nothing invisible), and a password typed twice (at least 6 characters, not the username; compared in Unicode NFC, so any keyboard's way of spelling the same letters works). It starts as your display name, and becomes your display name too unless you untick **Use it as my display name too**. Logged in, 🎨 Change me has **Same as my username**: on, the display name is the username; type another and it is your own (🔑 My login → **Use my username instead** goes back). On another device, **Log in** asks for the username and the password; that device gets a token of its own, and from then on it sends its copies to the player and brings back what the player's other devices sent: the newest copy of each island (owned by you there too), the look, name and basket changed last, every sticker and the most of everything done. An island you say goodbye to on one device goes from the others too; the keeper still has its copies. Logging out keeps that player's things on the device, apart from the guest's, for the next time they log in there. The keeper keeps passwords and tokens only as hashes (scrypt and SHA-256); after five wrong tries a name waits ten minutes, and a wrong password and a username with no login get the same answer. A player who forgot their password gets a new one on the admin page, which also takes a login away (every device logged in as them is logged out). Nobody plays without a login: a device nobody is logged in on shows only 🔑 Log in and ✨ Make my login on its title screen (and How to play, Sound and Full screen), with a note while the keeper is not online, and an invitation link waits there until someone logs in; logging out, or a login taken away, brings it back. Logging in needs the keeper to be online; once logged in, playing never does.
- **The ranking.** The keeper ranks the players with a login from the profiles their devices sent, whenever a page asks (any page, logged in or not, even with copies off): the top ten on each board by their display name and look, sharing a place on a tie, and the page's own player's place. Only real stickers count, and no count above ten million. It is live: a page showing it asks to watch it, keeps its connection open while it is on screen, and is sent the ranking again within a second of any change that makes it look different to that page (a player's profile, a login made or taken away, someone leaving or joining it). For that, a logged-in page sends what its player has done every 5 seconds while they play, instead of every few minutes. Players who left the ranking, or were taken out of it, are on no board; that choice is kept with the player at the keeper, so it holds on all their devices and through new passwords. Seeing it needs the keeper to be online. 🌟 Adventure Isle has a ranking of its own beside that one — boards for the monsters popped, camps freed, King Grumbles popped, stickers, steps and XP **on the isle**, from the isle's own world of each player that their devices send as they send the rest — and the ranking switches between the two worlds with one tap (on the isle, it opens on the isle's). Leaving the ranking leaves both. A keeper from before the isle's ranking says it needs an update when asked for it, there and in its terminal.
- **Islands open now.** A page hosting an island that friends can visit tells the keeper about it, and stays connected while it is open, so the keeper's list (kept in memory, never on disk) is always the islands open right now; any page asks for it in **Visit a friend**. A keeper running older code does not know the list: the page says it needs an update.
- **TV broadcasts.** The admin page's **TV broadcast** puts a show on every TV in the game, as channel 📡 Live, for every page connected to the keeper (logged-in players on screen), told as it starts, and as they connect while it is on. Paste a **YouTube link** (a watch, youtu.be, shorts or live link, a t= starting it there) and every TV plays it from YouTube's privacy-enhanced player, all at the same place in it and round again at its end; the admin page asks YouTube's player for its title and length, and turns away a video its owner does not let play elsewhere. Or pick **a video on this computer** (anything the browser plays): it never leaves the computer as a file. The admin page plays it (muted there, Play it again and again ticked or not) and is the TV station, a PeerJS peer each TV watching it calls for its picture and sound, live over WebRTC, so pausing or skipping there pauses or skips every TV; it needs Chrome or Firefox, and the page open while it is on air (it tells the keeper it is there every few seconds, and the video comes off half a minute after it is closed). Every TV watching takes its own stream from that computer, so a handful at home is fine, a classful asks a lot of its upload. **Take off air** ends it, and the TVs go back to their own channels. What is on air is kept in memory only. The keeper also tells every page who is on air from a [broadcast station](#what-you-can-do), one player at a time in the whole game, and the admin page's state includes them.
- **Screen time.** The admin page's Overview has **Lock all players**: every page connected to the keeper first shows a one-minute countdown over the game ("Screen time ends in 60 seconds. Time to finish up!"), then covers the game with "Screen time is over", taking no keys, clicks or touches and making no sound, and so does every page that connects while it is on (one that connects during the countdown counts down what is left of it). **Unlock all players** lets them play again, or calls off a countdown. The lock is kept in `lock.json` in the data folder, so a restart keeps it, and each page remembers the last word it heard, so a reload, or the keeper being away, does not unlock it; a locked page stays connected to hear when it is over.
- **Problems players tell of.** A player tells of one logged in and at level 5 on Adventure Isle (the keeper turns away a report from a page with no login, or from a player below that level there). 🐞 reports land in `reports/` in the data folder, a JSON file each and the picture of the screen beside it as a JPEG, and on the admin page under **Problem reports** in the **CTB** tab: what they wrote, the picture (bigger in a new tab), the island and device, and the page's latest errors, to mark resolved or delete. The keeper keeps the newest 200, takes six an hour from each device, and prints a line as each comes in. Each goes to CTB too, kept beside the player's ideas (see [Players' ideas](#ctb)), outside the ideas' own limits; and when CTB starts, it takes every open one it has not had yet, such as those from before it read them.
- **Players and invitations.** A logged-in page stays connected while it is on screen and says its player is playing now (kept in memory only, like the open islands), so up to 128 pages can be connected at once. The players list is every login's display name and look, under an id made for the list rather than the folder their copies are in. An invitation goes only to the pages of that player that are on screen right now, and only from a logged-in page; an island owner's carries a pass from the island that lets one friend in without the passcode within half an hour. Leaving the list is kept with the player, like leaving the ranking. A keeper running older code does not know the list: the page says it needs an update.
- **Bringing what was there before.** Everything kids made before logins exist comes along:
  - The device where a player makes their login: everything on it becomes the login (and the keeper's copies from it, sent before logins were needed).
  - Another device they played on as a guest: when they log in there, the game asks whether what that guest made is theirs. If it is, its islands, stickers, basket and what it did join the login, on that device and at the keeper (its copies there from before logins were needed, proved by that device's key and the new login's token), and the guest there starts afresh.
  - A device that is gone, say a replaced tablet: on the admin page, **Move into this login** puts its copies into a player's login, and every device logged in as them gets its islands.
  - Logins made in the days of secret pictures keep working on the devices logged in to them, which ask for a password the next time they reach the keeper; the admin page can set one too.
  - Logins from before usernames went by the made-up name: the keeper makes it their username when it starts, so they log in as before. Two of one name keep it both, told apart by their passwords.

```bash
export ZAI_API_KEY=your-key    # from https://z.ai
```

For the local standby (it falls back to it on its own):

```bash
brew install ollama
```

```bash
brew services start ollama
```

```bash
ollama pull gemma3:4b
```

The terminal says `AI friend: Pip 🤖 is awake` (or why it is asleep: then it is off the players list as playing, and checks again every minute).

- **What it is.** One more player, run by `server/buddy.js` with the game's own engine: it joins an island the way a page does, peer to peer through the same signaling service as the [island keeper](#the-island-keeper) (`server/dialer.js`, the calling side of what the keeper answers), or straight into an island of this dedicated server. Its words come from the model (Z.ai's, or the local Ollama's when Z.ai cannot answer), told the island, who is there and what was said, and answering as JSON (Z.ai's JSON mode, or Ollama's structured outputs), so it always says one short line and picks one thing to do: follow, stay, an emote, build a stamp, pet a pet, or go home. Walking, flying, helping a dizzy friend up and bopping monsters are its own, without the model.
- **Where it goes.** Invited, through the keeper's players list (only with the keeper online), to as many as two islands at once. By itself, every few minutes while it has a free place, to an island on the list of open islands with somebody on it and no passcode, the fewest players first, for a quarter of an hour or so (longer while friends talk with it). It does not go back by itself to an island it was sent home from that day, nor for a while to one where it was told bye. Never to the [CTB island](#ctb), CTB's office, invited or not (it says so when invited). Never to the shared isle, 🌟 Adventure Isle, however it is asked — not invited, not by itself, not to a stage just conquered either: that one is everybody's adventure, so it leaves it to the children.
- **Its own island.** On the server that runs it, it keeps an island of its own, saved in `ai-friend-island.json` in the keeper's data folder (kept over a restart, under the same code, with it as its owner). With the keeper online it is also hosted peer to peer, as a page hosts an island, and is on the keeper's list of open islands, so the game on GitHub Pages can visit it too; without a keeper, only pages of that server see it. When nobody is there it builds something every 6 to 12 minutes, after asking the model what (and in what colour and size), at a flat spot near where everyone comes in, clearing trees and flowers off it first; with friends there it plays with them, and builds on when they ask it to or once nobody has talked with it for a while. It stops when there is no room left.
- **Settings and watching it.** The **AI friend** part of the [admin page](http://localhost:8747/admin/) turns it on or off, names it (from its next visit on), picks one of the models Z.ai has, keeps it to invitations or lets it visit by itself, sets on how many islands it may be at once, and whether it has an island of its own; they apply at once and are saved in `ai-friend.json` in the keeper's data folder. It also shows whether it is awake (or why not), what it is talking with (Z.ai falling back to the local Ollama, and when one of them is down while the other still answers), what it is doing right now on each island it is on (building what, layer by layer, following whom, bopping a monster, thinking what to say or build), its own island with what it is building and has built there, the islands it is on with who is there and what is said, how many answers its model gave and how fast, **Token usage** by each model (today, the last 7 and 30 days and all of it, the tokens sent and answered with and in how many answers, as each model counts them, and what they cost at Z.ai's list prices (Ollama is free); kept a day at a time in `ai-friend-usage.json` beside its settings), and what it did lately, with a line to **Say** on each island, which it says there as its own words (the model sees it as something it said, and it is marked as from the admin page there), and **Send home** for each island (it does not go back there by itself for two hours). Until settings are saved there, they come from the environment: `KIDS_WORLD_AI=off` turns it off, `KIDS_WORLD_AI_WANDER=off` keeps it to invitations, `KIDS_WORLD_AI_HOME=off` gives it no island of its own, `KIDS_WORLD_AI_NAME` names it, `KIDS_WORLD_ZAI_MODEL` picks another Z.ai model and `KIDS_WORLD_ZAI_URL` points Z.ai somewhere else (or `KIDS_WORLD_ZAI_API_KEY` instead of `ZAI_API_KEY` for its key); `KIDS_WORLD_AI_MODEL` picks the local standby's model and `KIDS_WORLD_AI_URL` points at Ollama somewhere else.

## CTB

`npm start` also runs **CTB**, a company of workers who do real work on this project, with Z.ai's GLM models, as the [AI friend](#the-ai-friend) has (the same `ZAI_API_KEY`), but by default the best of them, GLM-5.3, rather than the AI friend's small, fast one, and only those: the local Ollama the AI friend falls back to is too small for real work, so while Z.ai cannot answer, CTB is closed and waits. Their tasks are the project's GitHub issues, read and written with the [GitHub CLI](https://cli.github.com) logged in on the keeper's computer (`gh auth login`), in the repository this folder's `origin` points at.

- **The task board.** A task is an open issue labelled `ctb`, written by the account `gh` is logged in as (anyone else's is never taken, so nobody can hand the workers work on a public repository). Add one in the **CTB** part of the [admin page](http://localhost:8747/admin/) (a title and what it is about), or on GitHub. Labels say where each stands: `ctb: working`, `ctb: pr` once its pull request is open, `ctb: stuck` when a worker could not finish (with why, in a comment; take the label off to have them try again). The labels are made the first time CTB opens.
- **Proposals.** With nothing to do, a worker now and then (every 6 hours at most, three waiting at most) thinks of a task and files it as an issue labelled `ctb: proposal`: short, and always in one shape — a **TL;DR**, what to change, and how to tell it is done. Nothing is done with it until it is labelled **`ctb: approved`**, on GitHub or with **Approve** on the admin page: that is the go-ahead, and it becomes a task (only the repository's own people can label an issue). **Turn down** closes it.
- **Doing a task.** A free worker takes the oldest task, comments that they are on it, and works in a git worktree of their own (`ctb-work/` in the keeper's data folder) on a branch `ctb/issue-<n>` from `origin/main`, one step at a time: the model answers each step as JSON naming a tool — list a folder, read a file, search, write or edit a file, run one test file with `node --test`, look over the diff, and done or give up (`server/ctb-agent.js`). Paths stay inside the worktree, and running a test file is the only command; but the tests run what the model wrote, as tests do. When it says done, all the unit tests run (not the end-to-end ones); a failure goes back to the worker to fix, twice at most. Then they commit as themselves (`Ada (CTB)`), push the branch and open a **pull request** that closes the issue, saying what they changed and how they tested it. Nothing reaches `main` until someone merges it (and the pre-push hook runs every test on it then). When Z.ai is busy (overloaded or down for a moment: a 429 or a 5xx, not out of credit), the worker waits and asks again, 5 seconds, then 15, 30, a minute, two and four (longer when Z.ai asks for longer), before those count as the model failing; five failures in a row and the task is stuck. A task has 100 steps; with nothing changed by 40 of them the worker is told to make the change, and from 60 it may not look around any more until it does. One a worker cannot finish keeps its work: what it changed is committed (`Work so far on #<n>`, with why it stopped) and pushed to its branch, with no pull request, and the next try starts from there, main merged in, told what the last one said. A worker out of steps first writes a note for the next try (what is done, what is left, what to check), kept with the work, and that next try is taken at once, up to two more times, before the task is marked stuck. A task the server stopping cuts off (a restart does not wait for a worker) carries on where it was when it is taken again: its worktree is left as it was, and its steps so far go back to the model from its run log. Each worker finishes its own task before taking another: one it ran out of steps on, or that a restart cut off, is taken again by that same worker, even with older tasks waiting, and by nobody else; a pull request or getting stuck frees it for the next.
- **The island.** The workers sit at their desks on the CTB island, an island of this server (kept in `ctb-island.json` in the keeper's data folder, and with the keeper online hosted peer to peer and on its list of open islands), saying what they take, what they are doing every few steps, and when they open a pull request or get stuck. A child who talks to them gets told what they are working on, answered in Korean when the child writes Korean. `<game>/?ctb` opens it for anyone, logged in or not (not logged in, under a name they must type first): the page finds it on the list of open islands as the one island the keeper's own computer marks as open to watch (a page's island never is). Its owner is one login, by username in the settings (izeye unless changed): the keeper finds that login and the island knows it by the hash every page of that login tells islands, so their page owns it as it comes, nobody else ever does, and away, it has none (an island from before, owned by a worker, stops being so).
- **Players' ideas.** A logged-in player at level 5 or more on Adventure Isle sends an idea for the game from 💌 (the title screen, the top bar, 💬, Help or Settings) (500 characters at most, three an hour, five waiting at once); the keeper keeps it in `wishes/` in its data folder and tells CTB, and the next worker in turn reads it with Z.ai and says yes or no to the player, in their own language, in a sentence or two, with a score out of 100 (the same rules as for the workers' own proposals, whose score is in their issue too). The answer goes to that player's pages open now (a toast, and their list of ideas), or waits for them, and the admin page lists every idea with its answer. A yes is filed as a proposal, labelled `ctb: request` too, in the same short shape as the workers' own, and credited to the player by the name they play under (never their username, which they log in with; as plain text, so a name makes no link or @mention) — the pull request that makes it credits them too; like theirs, nothing is built until it is approved. Every look at the board then reads those issues again (one `gh` call) and tells the player each step it took since, only forward: picked (approved), being built (`ctb: working`), stuck, being checked (`ctb: pr`), in the game (closed as done, once its pull request is merged) or not picked (closed as not planned). An idea the model or GitHub fails on waits for the next look. A 🐞 problem report is read the same way, with what the page knew (where the player was, the device, the latest errors; never the island's name or code) and what its picture shows: Z.ai's vision model (`glm-4.6v`, as the workers' own models read only text) describes it in words first, told what the player wrote, and the worker reads that, so a picture alone is read too (one it fails on waits for the next look): a real problem in the game is filed as a proposal labelled `ctb: bug` too, crediting the player for finding it, and anything else gets a kind answer; the player hears each step, said as a fix.
- **Settings and watching.** The **CTB** part of the admin page turns it on or off, sets how many workers there are (1 to 4, each working on a task of their own at once), the island's owner (a username), their model (`glm-5.3` until another is picked), and whether and how often they propose tasks; saved in `ctb.json` in the keeper's data folder (before that, `KIDS_WORLD_CTB=off` turns it off and `KIDS_WORLD_CTB_MODEL` picks their model). In its own tabs it shows each worker's task and their steps with what they were thinking, the task board, the proposals, their pull requests, the tokens their models took and what they cost (`ctb-usage.json`), and what happened lately. **🔍 Model runs** lists every talk a worker had with its model — a task step by step, an idea read, a proposal — newest first, everyone's or one worker's (🔍 on a worker opens that tab at their newest): open one to see what the model was given (the system prompt, the task, every tool result as it was sent), what it thought and answered (its reasoning, its answer as it came, why it stopped), how long each answer took and its tokens, failures and all, followed live while it goes on; **Download transcript** and **Copy transcript** give it as plain text, to read or to hand to another model to find out what went wrong. The runs are kept in `ctb-runs/` in the keeper's data folder, the newest 60 (`server/ctb-runs.js`). `KIDS_WORLD_PAGES_URL` changes the game's address in the island's link.

## How it works

```
 player's browser                           host (a browser, or server/server.js)
┌───────────────────────────┐  join, moves,  ┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Game: world copy, you,    │  edits, pets,  │ Room: players, tokens, rules       │
│   friends, animals        │ ─────────────► │   the island (128 to 256 across)   │
│ Renderer (three.js):      │                │   edits → checks → everyone        │
│   chunks, sky, characters │ ◄───────────── │   animals, clock, weather, sprouts │
│ UI, input, sound          │ welcome, edits,│   crops on farmland, and the rain  │
└───────────────────────────┘  moves, animals└────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- `public/js/shared/` is the engine, shared by the browser and the server: blocks, the world and its compact encoding (a whole cozy island is about 50 KB, a huge one about 160 KB), island generation, stamps and tents, walking, swimming and bouncing, aiming, the tools, the animals and riding them, pets, the vehicles (driving them, the digger's drill, mine carts on rails, and flying), the monsters, adventure islands (their camps, flags and King Grumble), tower defense islands (the road, the pads and towers, and the waves), the video arcade's mini-games, gashapon machines and their capsule toys, pianos and their songs, TVs and their channels, broadcast stations, drawing boards, their pictures and your gallery, farming (crops growing on farmland, and the rain and water close by watering it), time and weather, TV broadcasts, the words kids can use, the stickers and the ranking's boards, the shop's prices and gear, the list of open islands, invitations, the island's song in pieces, and the room. It has no DOM dependencies.
- Changes appear at once for the player who made them and are sent to the host, which checks them (inside the island, a known block, not inside another player, allowed by the island's rules) and passes them to everyone. Undo only changes blocks that nobody has changed since.
- Pets are no island's: a pet is part of its owner's look, and every page moves each pet on the island itself, after its owner as that page sees them, the way it moves name tags. A pet is right beside its owner on every screen however their moves come in, and the host only passes on who petted whose pet.
- The renderer builds each 16×16 chunk into meshes: faces with soft corner shadows and smooth light (sunlight and lamp light spread block by block, Minecraft style), instanced studs on top, flowers drawn as crossed pictures that sway, fruit and shells that turn to face you, and animated water. Characters are built from shapes in code and drawn as glossy toys (three.js's physical material, with a clear coat, reflecting a studio environment, and a rim of sky light); at the better 🖼️ Picture qualities the sun casts their shadows onto the chunks (three's shadow map, read in the chunks' own shaders), and each picture goes through three.js's post-processing: ambient occlusion (GTAO, worked out from the picture's own depth on a limited number of pixels) and, at Best, a depth-of-field blur focused on you. Every texture, icon and character is drawn in code; the only image files are the favicon and the Home Screen icon drawn from it (`npm run icon`).
- The transport is interchangeable: an in-page loopback (playing alone, and the host's own player), WebRTC data channels (peer to peer, with big messages split into pieces), or WebSocket (dedicated server).

## Tests

```bash
npm test
```

`npm test` (`scripts/test.mjs`) runs every test file at once, as many at a time as the computer has cores for, and shares the end-to-end tests out between six processes, three at a time, each with a browser and servers of its own; whatever took longest last time starts first. A file that passes gets a line, one that fails everything it printed, and a failed end-to-end test the command to run it again by itself. `npm test -- --touch` runs the end-to-end tests a second time with no mouse, as on a touch screen; with files or flags (`npm test -- test/sea.test.js`), it is plain `node --test` on just those. Running more at once is slower, not faster: a Mac has only a few performance cores, and on the rest a test runs several times as slowly, long enough for anything timed on the wall clock to run out.

CI runs no tests (there they took a quarter of an hour; here, all of them with a mouse and without one, about two and a half minutes). They run before every push to `main` instead: `npm install` turns on a pre-push hook (`.githooks/`) that tests the commit being pushed, on a clean copy of it, and stops the push if anything fails. The end-to-end tests run twice there, with a mouse and without one, as on a touch screen. The same by hand, for `HEAD` or any commit:

```bash
npm run check
```

- On a computer with a mouse the game is not in touch mode; on a touch screen, or a machine with no mouse, it is, with the thumbstick and the touch buttons on screen. To run the end-to-end tests that way, which matters for anything that moves the buttons or words a hint for touch (`npm run check` does both):

  ```bash
  E2E_NO_MOUSE=1 npm test
  ```

- A machine with no GPU, like a CI runner, draws in software on a slow CPU, and a frame can take seconds, so anything the tests wait for that is timed on the wall clock can run out between two frames there and never on a fast computer. On a Mac, keeping Chrome to the efficiency cores comes close to that:

  ```bash
  CI=1 E2E_NO_MOUSE=1 taskpolicy -c background npm test
  ```

- Every page a test opened is closed after it, passed or failed. A page left open keeps drawing its island in software, and the tests after it would take about three times as long, long enough to time out. An error in a page fails the test it came in, and only that one.
- The pages the end-to-end tests open are in a hurry (`window.kidsWorldHurry`, `public/js/hurry.js`): the waits a player never notices, before the keeper is first called, between saves of the island and between asking for a list again, are a tenth as long. A dialog popping in is finished at once before a click, instead of waited for.

## Privacy

- No analytics or cookies, and no account beyond the login you make. Your character, settings, basket, stickers and islands are kept in your browser's `localStorage`, each logged-in player's apart from the guest's. An island's song is kept in the browser's IndexedDB, and goes only to the players on that island while you play it: never to the keeper, nor to your other devices.
- While the [island keeper](#the-island-keeper) is online, it gets a copy of your islands, and of your name, look, basket, what you've done and your stickers; never your settings or tokens. Whoever runs the keeper can see and download those copies. With a login, your display name, look and those counts are in the ranking, which anyone can see, until you leave it; while you play, what you have done goes to the keeper every few seconds, for it. ⚙️ Settings → **Safe copies** turns this off for your browser; logged in, copies always go, as that is what a login is for.
- The [AI friend](#the-ai-friend) hears what is said on an island it is visiting, and sees who is there and the island, as any player does. That goes only to the language model that answers it — Z.ai's, or the local Ollama's when Z.ai cannot — never to any other service, and nothing of it is kept: it is forgotten when the visit ends. While it is there, the admin page on that computer shows it too.
- An idea for the game, and a problem told of, go to the island keeper with who sent it (a login is needed for both), and an idea or a problem told of from there to Z.ai for a CTB worker to read (a problem with the page's latest errors, device and screen, and the picture that came with it, which Z.ai's vision model describes in words for the worker); one they take becomes a public GitHub issue in their own words, crediting the player by their display name (never their username), as its pull request does.
- [CTB](#ctb)'s workers send the project's code, the tasks' issues and the test output to the language model they work with, Z.ai's, and what they make to GitHub as branches, pull requests and issue comments under the account `gh` is logged in as. On the CTB island they hear what is said to them only to answer with what they are working on: nothing said there goes to a model. Anyone with the `?ctb` link can come to that island without logging in, under their display name.
- A 🐞 problem report goes only to the island keeper, and only when you send one: what you wrote, the picture of your screen (or the one you picked) if you leave it on, your display name, the island's name, code and kind (never its passcode), the size of the screen, the browser's user agent and the page's latest errors. Whoever runs the keeper sees it on the admin page.
- Watching a YouTube video on a TV's 📡 Live channel loads it from `youtube-nocookie.com`, YouTube's privacy-enhanced player, on that device. A video from the keeper's computer goes from it straight to each TV watching, peer to peer, through the signaling server as islands do.
- A login is a username, kept at the island keeper as typed, and a password kept there as a hash, with a hash of each logged-in device's token. Pages send none of it, nor anything else, until the keeper has proved who it is.
- In peer-to-peer play the signaling server sees peer ids and connection setup data (which include IP addresses), never the game. Anyone with an island's code can visit it while it's open, unless the owner closes it to new visitors or gives it a passcode.
- An island open to friends is on the list of open islands, which anyone can see: its name, code, kind, size and how many are playing, never who. Peer to peer, the host's page tells the [island keeper](#the-island-keeper) while it is online, whether or not Safe copies are on; the keeper keeps the list in memory only. Playing alone (Friends can visit off) keeps an island off it.
